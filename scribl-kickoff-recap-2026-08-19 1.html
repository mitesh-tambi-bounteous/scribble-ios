What was decided

The phase is eight weeks at roughly a third of the original scope. Two weeks of Shape followed by three two-week sprints. Scope follows the budget and timeline rather than a fixed feature list. Velocity is the metric we report against, meaning how much working software the team delivers per sprint, measured sprint over sprint.

The clock starts Monday, August 24, 2026. The team gets its hardware Friday, August 21, which keeps Scribl from paying for internal onboarding.

iOS is the platform. One platform, not two, given the timeline and budget. Eric confirmed the Scribl team is on company iPhones, so an iOS build is what the people who need to try it can actually hold.

re:Invent is still the north star. No other external commitment is driving the date. There is no beta promise outstanding to investors.

The MVP gets smaller, not bigger. The feedback Scribl gathered from investors, board, and advisors was consistent, that the product needs less. Nothing gets added to scope. The work is to simplify, get the foundation right, and test hard.

Daily prompts stay Scribl's. Scribl's program team, made up of mental health professionals and educators, reviews every daily prompt, and that review is part of what sets the product apart. Claude drafts candidates and Scribl has final say. Claude generating prompts live is a fit inside walls and ad hoc family or friend groups, where it can work from who is in the group and what they have drawn together, but not for the curated daily prompt.

Reactions are in for the MVP. Comments are not. An emoji or reaction is enough sentiment for the first thirty to sixty days, and whether people ask for comments is itself a useful test result. The wall owner toggle Eric raised, along with an AI check on comments before they post, is a direction we like rather than something scoped today.

Analytics moves into launch scope. Instrumentation goes in with the build rather than after it, because it is what tells Scribl which features to back. It comes with a simple, readable dashboard, not a SQL exercise. Matt is sending the specific metrics Scribl already drafted.

Eric Rice is the backlog owner. He owns and prioritizes, and decisions come back inside two business days. In practice Eric checks with Matt and turns them around faster than that.

Christina takes the hardest screens. She picks the screens she most wants her fingerprint on and designs those fully. Rob replicates her theme across the remaining screens in the prototype so she can react to a running app rather than a spec. That runs on a required weekly sync of 20 to 30 minutes, with Eric on the invite.

The keepsake work consolidates under Bounteous. After the conversations with Mission Cloud and with James, the AWS account rep, Scribl decided one team is better than two on a feature this central. Scribl is keeping the relationship with Mission open.

Claude runs on Bedrock, consistent with the AWS agreement and what AWS is funding.

Demos are Wednesday mornings. Wednesday works for the India time zone overlap and sits midweek. Readouts stay light. The demo is the readout, and no elaborate deck goes with it. Daily standups run in the morning, and Matt and Eric are both welcome on them.

Tooling. Jira for the backlog and boards, with Scribl given access. Google Drive for documents. Nothing in the Microsoft ecosystem. The code starts in a Bounteous repository and migrates to a Scribl repository later.

The capacity fact

This governs every scope conversation from here.

Roughly 21 person-weeks sit inside the three build sprints, about 7 per sprint. Feature work is serial behind one iOS engineer. The order we build in decides what exists at week 8.

This is not a reason to trim ambition. It is the reason the prioritization workshop in Shape week 1 matters more than any other meeting in the eight weeks.
